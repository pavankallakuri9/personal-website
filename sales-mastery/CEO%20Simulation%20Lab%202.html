The Boardroom: Amber Crown Brewing Co.

You are the founding CEO. You have five years, a fixed pool of capital, three entrenched rivals and a board that judges you on value created, not volume shipped. Every lever is connected. Decide like it's real.

The situation

Amber Crown Brewing Co. is a newly incorporated lager company. Investors have committed growth equity and two banks have offered term debt. You must build a brewery, choose how beer reaches the consumer, set price against three incumbents, and deliver a business the board considers sustainable and value-creating by the end of Year 5.

"Anyone can buy market share. Anyone can cut costs until the brand dies. Your job is to find the narrow path where customers, cash and capability reinforce one another." — Chair of the Board

Intelligence on rivals

  • Valor defends share fiercely. If a newcomer gains real share at a visibly lower price, expect a price response.
  • Titan does not cut price; it outspends on brand when someone attacks the premium tier.
  • Kaya lives on thin margins and will follow anyone who prices near it.

Economics of a bottle

  • Excise duty: $0.10 per bottle, paid by the brewer. The Finance Ministry has signalled a hike from Year 3.
  • Retailer margin: 6–25% of shelf price depending on channel.
  • Distributor margin: you set it. Higher margin buys push and shelf space.
  • Ingredients: malt, hops, adjuncts, water. Better recipes cost more and drive repeat purchase.
  • Packaging is the single largest cost line. Glass, returnables, cans, or build your own furnace.
  • Commodity risk: barley, glass and energy prices are volatile. Hedging is available at a cost.
  • Cost inflation: about 4% a year on most inputs.

Your plan is also re-run silently under recession, a severe commodity shock and aggressive competitors. Plans that only work in good weather are penalised.

The dilemmas you must resolve

  • Scale vs. risk: a big plant lowers unit cost but an idle plant destroys cash.
  • Price vs. proof: can your quality and brand justify your price against Titan and Valor?
  • Leverage: debt is cheaper than equity until the covenants bite.
  • Route to market: bars build brands, supermarkets build volume, kiosks build reach. Each has different margins and payment terms.
  • Working capital: generous credit wins distributors and quietly drains cash.
  • Integration: owning the glass furnace is a scale bet, not a cost trick.
  • People: under-paying a brewery workforce has consequences.
  • Sustainability: regulators are tightening on waste water and packaging.
